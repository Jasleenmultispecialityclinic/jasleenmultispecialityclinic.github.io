Healthy Weight Management

Weight management is more than simply losing weight. A healthy approach focuses on sustainable eating habits, regular physical activity, adequate sleep, mental well-being and management of medical conditions that may affect body weight.

At Jasleen Multi-Speciality Clinic in Behram, weight-related concerns can be assessed in the context of your overall health, lifestyle, medical history and individual goals.

When Can Weight Become a Health Concern?

Excess body weight can be associated with an increased risk of several health conditions, although health risks vary between individuals.

  • Type 2 diabetes
  • High blood pressure
  • Abnormal cholesterol and triglycerides
  • Fatty liver disease
  • Sleep-related breathing problems
  • Joint and mobility problems
  • Some cardiovascular conditions
  • PCOS-related metabolic concerns in some women

What Can Affect Body Weight?

Body weight is influenced by many factors. These may include:

  • Dietary habits
  • Physical activity
  • Sleep patterns
  • Stress and mental health
  • Genetics
  • Age and hormonal changes
  • Medical conditions
  • Some medicines
  • Social and environmental factors

Healthy Eating for Weight Management

A sustainable eating pattern generally emphasizes nutritious foods while reducing excessive intake of highly processed foods and added sugars.

  • Include plenty of vegetables.
  • Choose suitable whole grains and high-fibre foods.
  • Include appropriate protein sources.
  • Choose healthy fats in suitable portions.
  • Choose whole fruits rather than relying on sugary drinks.
  • Limit frequent sweets and highly processed snacks.
  • Pay attention to portion sizes.
  • Stay adequately hydrated.

Physical Activity

Regular physical activity can support weight management, cardiovascular health, muscle strength and overall well-being.

  • Walking
  • Cycling
  • Swimming
  • Strength training
  • Other enjoyable activities that can be maintained regularly

The appropriate amount and intensity of physical activity depends on your age, fitness level and medical conditions.

Weight Management and PCOS

Some women with PCOS also have overweight or obesity. For those individuals, appropriate weight management may improve some metabolic and reproductive features of PCOS.

Women with PCOS should receive an individualized plan rather than following an extreme or highly restrictive diet.

Weight Management and Thyroid Problems

Thyroid disorders can affect metabolism and body weight. If you have unexplained weight changes along with symptoms suggestive of thyroid disease, your doctor may recommend appropriate evaluation.

Why Crash Diets Are Not a Good Long-Term Strategy

Very restrictive diets can be difficult to maintain and may lead to repeated cycles of weight loss and regain. A sustainable plan that fits your lifestyle is generally more practical for long-term weight management.

When Should You Consult a Doctor?

Medical consultation may be helpful if you have significant unexplained weight gain or loss, difficulty managing weight despite lifestyle changes, or medical conditions such as diabetes, high blood pressure, PCOS or thyroid problems.

Your doctor can also review medicines and other factors that may be contributing to changes in weight.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is healthy weight management?

Healthy weight management focuses on sustainable eating habits, regular physical activity, adequate sleep and management of medical factors that can affect weight.

Can medical conditions cause weight gain?

Some medical conditions and medicines can contribute to weight changes. Depending on the situation, your healthcare professional may assess thyroid function, diabetes, medications and lifestyle factors.

Is crash dieting recommended?

Extreme crash diets are generally not a sustainable approach. A gradual, individualized and sustainable plan is usually more appropriate.

Can weight management help diabetes and PCOS?

For people with overweight or obesity, appropriate weight management may improve some metabolic and reproductive health outcomes, depending on the individual condition.
