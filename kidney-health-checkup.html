Why Is Kidney Health Important?

The kidneys help remove waste and excess fluid from the body and play an important role in maintaining fluid, electrolyte and blood pressure balance. Maintaining kidney health is an important part of overall healthcare.

Common Kidney-Related Problems

  • Chronic kidney disease
  • Kidney stones
  • Urinary tract problems
  • Kidney-related changes in blood or urine tests
  • Kidney problems associated with diabetes or high blood pressure

Risk Factors for Kidney Disease

  • Diabetes
  • High blood pressure
  • Family history of kidney disease
  • Older age
  • Obesity
  • Previous kidney problems
  • Long-term use of certain medicines

Possible Symptoms

Early kidney disease may not cause noticeable symptoms. Depending on the condition and severity, symptoms may include swelling of the legs, changes in urination, blood in urine, fatigue, nausea or other complaints.

Kidney Function Tests

When clinically indicated, a doctor may recommend blood and urine tests to assess kidney health. Common investigations may include serum creatinine, estimated kidney function and urine testing. Additional tests depend on the clinical situation.

Kidney Health and Diabetes

Diabetes is an important risk factor for kidney disease. Appropriate blood sugar control and regular medical monitoring can help reduce the risk of diabetes-related complications.

Kidney Health and High Blood Pressure

High blood pressure can affect kidney health, while kidney disease can also contribute to elevated blood pressure. Appropriate medical evaluation and blood pressure management are therefore important.

Healthy Habits for Kidney Health

  • Maintain good blood pressure control.
  • Manage diabetes when applicable.
  • Maintain a healthy body weight.
  • Stay physically active.
  • Drink fluids appropriately for your health condition.
  • Avoid unnecessary use of medicines, especially pain medicines.
  • Attend medical follow-up when kidney tests are abnormal.

Kidney Stones

Kidney stones can cause severe pain, commonly in the side or back, and may sometimes cause blood in the urine or urinary symptoms. Medical evaluation is recommended when symptoms suggest a kidney stone or another urinary condition.

When Should You Consult a Doctor?

  • Abnormal kidney function test results
  • Blood in urine
  • Persistent changes in urination
  • Swelling of the legs or face
  • Recurrent kidney stones
  • Known diabetes or hypertension with kidney concerns
  • Persistent pain in the side or back with urinary symptoms
