Kidney Health Checkup & Consultation

The kidneys help remove waste and excess fluid from the body and play important roles in maintaining fluid balance, blood pressure and other body functions.

Some kidney problems may develop gradually without obvious symptoms. Regular medical assessment can therefore be useful for people who have risk factors such as diabetes, high blood pressure or a previous history of kidney disease.

Who May Need Kidney Health Monitoring?

  • People with diabetes
  • People with high blood pressure
  • People with a previous history of kidney disease
  • People with recurrent urinary problems
  • People with a family history of certain kidney conditions
  • People with abnormal kidney-related blood or urine tests
  • People advised by their doctor to monitor kidney function

Common Kidney Function Tests

The tests recommended depend on the patient's symptoms, medical history and risk factors. Your doctor may consider:

  • Serum creatinine
  • Estimated kidney function
  • Urine routine examination
  • Urine protein or albumin testing when indicated
  • Blood pressure measurement
  • Blood glucose / HbA1c when appropriate
  • Ultrasound or other imaging when clinically indicated

Not every patient needs all of these investigations. Testing should be selected according to the individual's clinical condition.

Kidney Health and Diabetes

Diabetes can increase the risk of kidney disease over time. Appropriate blood glucose management and regular medical follow-up can help identify kidney-related problems early.

Kidney Health and Blood Pressure

High blood pressure and kidney health are closely related. Long-term uncontrolled blood pressure can contribute to kidney damage, while kidney problems can also affect blood pressure.

Urine Problems and Kidney Health

Changes such as painful urination, blood in urine, recurrent urinary infections, unusual changes in urine or swelling may require medical evaluation. The cause can vary and should be assessed clinically.

Healthy Habits for Kidney Health

  • Keep blood pressure under appropriate control.
  • Manage diabetes if present.
  • Maintain a healthy body weight.
  • Stay physically active as appropriate.
  • Follow a balanced eating pattern.
  • Avoid tobacco.
  • Take medicines only as advised by your healthcare professional.
  • Attend recommended medical follow-ups.

When Should You Seek Medical Advice?

Seek medical evaluation if you develop persistent swelling, blood in the urine, significant changes in urination, unexplained weakness or other concerning symptoms. Urgent assessment may be necessary for severe symptoms or sudden deterioration.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is a kidney health checkup important?

Some kidney problems may develop without obvious symptoms. Kidney health assessment can be particularly important for people with diabetes, high blood pressure, previous kidney problems or other risk factors.

Which tests are commonly used to assess kidney function?

Depending on the clinical situation, your doctor may recommend blood tests such as serum creatinine and estimated kidney function, urine testing and other investigations when appropriate.

Can diabetes affect kidney health?

Yes. Diabetes can increase the risk of kidney disease. Regular medical follow-up and appropriate kidney health monitoring may be recommended.

Can high blood pressure affect the kidneys?

Yes. Long-term high blood pressure can affect kidney health. Appropriate blood pressure control and medical follow-up are important.
