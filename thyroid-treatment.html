Thyroid Consultation & Treatment

The thyroid gland produces hormones that influence metabolism, energy, temperature regulation and several other body functions. Thyroid disorders can occur at any age and may cause a wide range of symptoms.

Symptoms of Hypothyroidism

Hypothyroidism can cause different symptoms, and some people may have few symptoms in the early stages.

  • Tiredness or low energy
  • Feeling unusually cold
  • Constipation
  • Dry skin
  • Hair changes
  • Weight gain or difficulty losing weight
  • Menstrual changes
  • Slow heart rate in some cases

These symptoms are not specific to thyroid disease and may have many other causes.

Symptoms of Hyperthyroidism

An overactive thyroid can cause symptoms related to increased metabolic activity.

  • Fast or irregular heartbeat
  • Feeling unusually warm
  • Excessive sweating
  • Trembling or shakiness
  • Anxiety or restlessness
  • Unintentional weight loss
  • Increased bowel movements
  • Difficulty sleeping

Thyroid Blood Tests

Thyroid function is commonly assessed using blood tests. Depending on the clinical situation, your doctor may recommend:

  • TSH
  • Free T4
  • Other thyroid-related tests when clinically indicated

Not every person needs every thyroid test. The appropriate tests depend on symptoms, medical history, previous reports and treatment status.

Thyroid Treatment

Treatment depends on the type and severity of thyroid disorder. Hypothyroidism is commonly treated with thyroid hormone replacement when treatment is indicated.

Hyperthyroidism may require a different treatment approach depending on its cause and severity.

Do not start, stop or change thyroid medication without medical advice.

Thyroid Medicine & TSH Monitoring

If you are taking thyroid medication, regular follow-up and appropriate blood testing may be required to determine whether the dose remains suitable.

The timing of repeat thyroid testing depends on the medication, dose changes, diagnosis and individual clinical circumstances.

Thyroid and Women's Health

Thyroid disorders can affect menstrual cycles, ovulation and fertility in some women. Women with persistent menstrual changes may require thyroid evaluation along with assessment for other possible causes.

Thyroid and PCOS

PCOS and thyroid disorders are separate conditions, although they can coexist. Irregular periods should not automatically be attributed to PCOS without appropriate evaluation.

Thyroid During Pregnancy

Thyroid function is particularly important during pregnancy. Women who have known thyroid disease or abnormal thyroid tests should discuss appropriate monitoring with their pregnancy healthcare professional.

When Should You Consult a Doctor?

Medical consultation may be appropriate if you have persistent symptoms suggestive of thyroid disease, an abnormal TSH report, a known thyroid condition requiring follow-up or concerns about thyroid medication.

Seek prompt medical attention for severe symptoms such as significant breathing difficulty, chest pain, fainting or a very rapid or irregular heartbeat.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are common symptoms of hypothyroidism?

Symptoms may include tiredness, feeling unusually cold, constipation, dry skin, weight changes and menstrual changes. Symptoms vary and can have other causes.

What blood tests are used for thyroid evaluation?

Thyroid evaluation commonly involves TSH and, when appropriate, free T4 or other thyroid-related tests based on the clinical situation.

Can thyroid problems affect periods?

Yes. Thyroid disorders can affect menstrual cycles and reproductive health in some women.

Should thyroid medicine be stopped when TSH becomes normal?

No. Thyroid medication should not be stopped or changed without medical advice. Treatment decisions depend on the diagnosis, medication, laboratory results and individual clinical situation.
