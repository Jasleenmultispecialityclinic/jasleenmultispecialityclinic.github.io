Healthy Pregnancy Diet & Nutrition

Good nutrition during pregnancy supports the health of both mother and developing baby. Pregnancy nutrition should focus on a varied, balanced eating pattern rather than a highly restrictive diet.

Individual nutritional needs can vary depending on the stage of pregnancy, pre-pregnancy health, weight, blood sugar, anemia, thyroid problems and other medical conditions.

Foods to Include During Pregnancy

  • Vegetables: Include a variety of well-washed vegetables in regular meals.
  • Fruits: Choose a variety of fresh, properly washed fruits.
  • Whole grains: Include suitable whole-grain and high-fibre carbohydrate sources.
  • Protein-rich foods: Dal, beans, pulses, eggs, dairy products and other appropriate protein sources can be included according to dietary preference.
  • Dairy or fortified alternatives: Useful sources of calcium and other nutrients when appropriately selected.
  • Nuts and seeds: Can be included in suitable portions if there is no allergy or medical restriction.

Important Nutrients During Pregnancy

Several nutrients are particularly important during pregnancy. Your healthcare professional may recommend dietary changes or prenatal supplements depending on your individual needs.

  • Folate / folic acid
  • Iron
  • Calcium
  • Vitamin D
  • Iodine
  • Protein
  • Other vitamins and minerals as clinically required

Do not start high-dose vitamins, herbal products or other supplements without discussing them with your pregnancy healthcare professional.

Foods to Avoid or Handle Carefully

Pregnancy increases the importance of food safety. To reduce the risk of foodborne illness:

  • Avoid raw or undercooked meat and poultry.
  • Avoid raw or undercooked eggs unless safely prepared.
  • Avoid unpasteurized milk or dairy products.
  • Wash fruits and vegetables thoroughly.
  • Use safe drinking water.
  • Keep raw and cooked foods separated.
  • Follow proper refrigeration and food-storage practices.

Caffeine During Pregnancy

Tea, coffee, energy drinks, cola and some other products contain caffeine. Caffeine intake should be limited during pregnancy.

If you regularly consume tea, coffee or other caffeinated drinks, discuss your total daily intake with your healthcare professional so that it can be kept within recommended limits.

Pregnancy Diet and Anemia

Iron requirements increase during pregnancy. A balanced diet containing iron-rich foods can support nutritional needs, while your doctor may recommend iron supplementation when appropriate.

If you have anemia or low hemoglobin, treatment should be based on the cause and your laboratory results rather than diet alone.

Healthy Weight Gain During Pregnancy

Weight gain during pregnancy is expected, but the appropriate amount depends on factors including pre-pregnancy body mass index, pregnancy type and individual health.

Pregnancy is not the time for crash dieting or intentional rapid weight loss. Your pregnancy healthcare professional can guide you regarding appropriate nutrition and weight monitoring.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I eat during pregnancy?

A balanced variety of vegetables, fruits, whole grains, protein-rich foods and appropriate dairy or fortified alternatives can form part of a healthy pregnancy eating pattern.

Which nutrients are important during pregnancy?

Important nutrients include folate, iron, iodine, calcium, vitamin D, protein and other nutrients. Your doctor may recommend prenatal supplements based on your individual needs.

Which foods should be avoided during pregnancy?

Avoid foods with a higher risk of foodborne infection, such as raw or undercooked meat and eggs and unpasteurized products. Proper food hygiene is also important.

Can I drink tea or coffee during pregnancy?

Caffeine intake should be limited during pregnancy. Discuss your total daily caffeine intake with your healthcare professional.
