Pregnancy Diet Guide in Behram

General nutrition guidance for a healthy pregnancy and the well-being of mother and baby.

Why Nutrition Matters During Pregnancy

A balanced diet during pregnancy helps provide essential nutrients for the mother's health and the baby's growth and development. Nutritional requirements change during pregnancy, so individual dietary needs should be discussed with a qualified healthcare professional.

Foods to Include During Pregnancy

  • Fresh vegetables and seasonal fruits
  • Whole grains and other high-fibre foods
  • Protein-rich foods such as pulses, beans, eggs and appropriately prepared lean protein
  • Milk, curd and other suitable dairy products
  • Nuts and seeds in appropriate portions
  • Healthy sources of fats
  • Adequate fluids and water

Important Nutrients During Pregnancy

Pregnancy increases the need for several nutrients. Depending on individual requirements, a healthcare professional may recommend supplements such as folic acid, iron, calcium, vitamin D or other nutrients.

Do not start or stop pregnancy supplements without discussing them with your doctor.

Foods and Drinks to Avoid or Limit

  • Alcohol should be avoided during pregnancy.
  • Unpasteurized milk or dairy products should be avoided.
  • Raw or undercooked meat, eggs and seafood should be avoided.
  • Unwashed fruits and vegetables should be cleaned properly.
  • High-mercury fish should be avoided.
  • Excessive caffeine intake should be avoided.
  • Highly processed foods should be limited.

Food Safety During Pregnancy

Wash fruits and vegetables properly, cook meat and eggs thoroughly, keep raw and cooked foods separate and maintain good hand and kitchen hygiene.

Hydration During Pregnancy

Adequate fluid intake is important during pregnancy. Water should generally be the main source of hydration. Individual fluid needs may vary depending on weather, activity, vomiting and other medical conditions.

Pregnancy Diet May Need Individualization

There is no single pregnancy diet that is suitable for everyone. Women with diabetes, thyroid disease, anemia, severe vomiting, high blood pressure or other medical conditions may need individualized nutritional advice.

When Should You Consult Your Doctor?

  • Persistent vomiting or difficulty keeping food down
  • Significant weight loss
  • Symptoms of anemia or severe weakness
  • Gestational diabetes or high blood sugar
  • High blood pressure during pregnancy
  • Any concern about nutrition or fetal growth
