What Is Anemia?

Anemia is a condition in which the blood does not have enough healthy red blood cells or hemoglobin to adequately carry oxygen to the body's tissues. The underlying cause should be identified before deciding on treatment.

Common Symptoms of Anemia

  • Tiredness and weakness
  • Shortness of breath, especially with exertion
  • Dizziness or light-headedness
  • Pale skin
  • Headache
  • Reduced exercise tolerance
  • Fast heartbeat in some cases

Common Causes of Anemia

  • Iron deficiency
  • Vitamin B12 deficiency
  • Folate deficiency
  • Blood loss
  • Heavy menstrual bleeding
  • Chronic medical conditions
  • Problems affecting red blood cell production

Iron Deficiency Anemia

Iron deficiency is one of the common causes of anemia. It can occur because of inadequate dietary intake, increased requirements, poor absorption or blood loss. In women, heavy menstrual bleeding can be an important cause of iron deficiency.

How Is Anemia Diagnosed?

A complete blood count (CBC) is commonly used to evaluate hemoglobin and red blood cell parameters. Depending on the findings and clinical history, a doctor may recommend additional tests such as iron studies, ferritin, vitamin B12, folate or other investigations.

Anemia Treatment

Treatment depends on the underlying cause and severity of anemia. Depending on the individual situation, treatment may include dietary improvement, iron or other nutritional supplementation, treatment of blood loss or management of an underlying medical condition.

The appropriate medicine and dose should be selected after medical evaluation. Not every case of anemia is caused by iron deficiency.

Nutrition and Anemia

A balanced diet can provide important nutrients needed for healthy blood formation. Depending on individual dietary requirements, sources of iron, vitamin B12, folate and adequate protein may be included in the diet.

Anemia in Women

Women may develop anemia because of heavy or prolonged menstrual bleeding, pregnancy, increased nutritional requirements or other causes. Persistent low hemoglobin should be evaluated to identify the underlying reason.

Anemia During Pregnancy

Anemia can occur during pregnancy and may require appropriate screening and treatment. Pregnant women should follow their antenatal doctor's advice regarding iron, folic acid and other supplements.

When Should You Consult a Doctor?

  • Low hemoglobin on a blood test
  • Persistent tiredness or weakness
  • Heavy or prolonged menstrual bleeding
  • Shortness of breath or reduced exercise tolerance
  • Repeatedly low hemoglobin despite treatment
  • Anemia during pregnancy
  • Unexplained anemia
