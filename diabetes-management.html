What Is Diabetes?

Diabetes is a chronic condition in which blood glucose levels remain higher than normal. Long-term blood sugar control is important to reduce the risk of complications affecting the eyes, kidneys, nerves, heart and blood vessels.

Common Types of Diabetes

  • Type 1 diabetes
  • Type 2 diabetes
  • Gestational diabetes
  • Other specific forms of diabetes

Common Symptoms of High Blood Sugar

  • Increased thirst
  • Frequent urination
  • Increased hunger
  • Unexplained weight loss
  • Tiredness
  • Blurred vision
  • Slow healing of wounds

Diabetes Evaluation

Diabetes may be diagnosed using appropriate blood glucose testing. Depending on the clinical situation, a doctor may recommend tests such as fasting blood glucose, HbA1c or other investigations.

Diabetes Management

Diabetes management usually involves a combination of healthy eating, regular physical activity, appropriate weight management, blood sugar monitoring and medicines when required.

The treatment plan should be individualized according to the type of diabetes, blood glucose levels, age, other medical conditions and current medicines.

Healthy Lifestyle for Diabetes

  • Follow a balanced and appropriate diet.
  • Stay physically active according to your health status.
  • Maintain a healthy weight when appropriate.
  • Take prescribed medicines regularly.
  • Monitor blood glucose as advised.
  • Avoid smoking and limit alcohol as medically advised.
  • Attend regular medical follow-ups.

Diabetes Monitoring

Regular monitoring may include blood glucose testing and periodic HbA1c assessment. Depending on the duration and control of diabetes, your doctor may also recommend monitoring of blood pressure, kidney function, cholesterol, eyes and feet.

Diabetes and Other Health Conditions

Diabetes can occur together with conditions such as high blood pressure, high cholesterol, obesity and thyroid disorders. A complete medical assessment can help identify and manage associated health risks.

When Should You Consult a Doctor?

  • Repeatedly high blood sugar readings
  • New symptoms suggestive of diabetes
  • Abnormal HbA1c or blood glucose results
  • Frequent episodes of low blood sugar
  • Unexplained weight changes
  • Slow-healing wounds or recurrent infections
