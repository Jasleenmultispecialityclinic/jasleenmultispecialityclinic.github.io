A breast lump does not always mean breast cancer. Many breast lumps are benign (non-cancerous), such as cysts or fibroadenomas. However, every new breast lump should be examined by a qualified doctor for proper diagnosis.

Common Symptoms

  • A lump in one or both breasts
  • Breast swelling
  • Pain or tenderness
  • Change in breast size or shape
  • Nipple discharge
  • Skin dimpling or thickening
  • Redness or warmth of the breast
  • Swollen lymph nodes in the armpit

Possible Causes

  • Fibroadenoma
  • Breast cyst
  • Fibrocystic breast changes
  • Breast infection (Mastitis)
  • Breast abscess
  • Fat necrosis
  • Hormonal changes
  • Breast cancer (less common but important to rule out)

Diagnosis & Evaluation

  • Clinical Breast Examination
  • Breast Ultrasound
  • Mammography (when indicated)
  • FNAC (Fine Needle Aspiration Cytology)
  • Core Needle Biopsy (selected cases)
  • Blood Tests (if required)

Treatment Options

Treatment depends on the cause of the breast lump. Benign conditions may only require observation or medicines, while infections require antibiotics. Selected patients may need further imaging or biopsy for confirmation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is every breast lump cancer?

No. Most breast lumps are non-cancerous (benign), such as fibroadenomas or cysts. However, every new breast lump should be evaluated by a doctor.

When should I consult a doctor?

Consult a doctor immediately if you notice a new breast lump, bloody nipple discharge, skin dimpling, persistent swelling or changes in breast shape.

Can young women develop breast lumps?

Yes. Benign breast lumps like fibroadenomas are common in younger women and usually require proper evaluation.

Will I need surgery?

Not always. Many breast lumps only require observation, medicines or follow-up. Surgery is recommended only in selected cases.

How is a breast lump diagnosed?

Diagnosis may include a clinical examination, breast ultrasound, mammography and biopsy when necessary.
