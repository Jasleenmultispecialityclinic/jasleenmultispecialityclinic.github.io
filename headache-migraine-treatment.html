Headache & Migraine Consultation

Headache is a common symptom and can have many different causes. Migraine, tension-type headache and other primary headache disorders can cause recurrent symptoms, while some headaches may be related to another medical condition.

At Jasleen Multi-Speciality Clinic in Behram, recurrent headaches can be assessed based on the pattern of pain, associated symptoms, medical history and possible triggers.

Common Types of Headache

  • Migraine
  • Tension-type headache
  • Headache associated with dehydration or missed meals
  • Headache related to sleep problems or stress
  • Medication-related headache
  • Headaches caused by other medical conditions

Common Migraine Symptoms

Migraine symptoms vary from person to person. A migraine attack may include:

  • Moderate to severe headache
  • Throbbing or pulsating pain in some people
  • Nausea or vomiting
  • Sensitivity to light
  • Sensitivity to sound
  • Worsening of symptoms with routine physical activity
  • Visual or other neurological symptoms in some people

Common Migraine Triggers

Migraine triggers are different for different people. Possible triggers may include:

  • Stress
  • Irregular or insufficient sleep
  • Skipped meals
  • Dehydration
  • Hormonal changes
  • Bright lights or strong smells
  • Changes in routine
  • Certain foods or beverages in some individuals

Keeping a headache diary can help identify personal patterns and possible triggers.

Headache Diary

If headaches occur repeatedly, record:

  • Date and time of headache
  • Duration
  • Location of pain
  • Severity
  • Associated symptoms
  • Food and fluid intake
  • Sleep pattern
  • Possible stress or other triggers
  • Medicines taken and response

Lifestyle Measures That May Help

  • Maintain regular sleep and wake times.
  • Do not regularly skip meals.
  • Stay adequately hydrated.
  • Manage stress where possible.
  • Take regular breaks from prolonged screen use.
  • Identify and avoid personal migraine triggers.
  • Maintain regular physical activity when appropriate.

Medication Overuse Headache

Frequent use of pain-relieving medicines for headaches can sometimes contribute to medication-overuse headache.

If you need headache medicines very frequently, medical evaluation can help determine a safer long-term treatment strategy.

When Should a Headache Be Evaluated Urgently?

Some headache patterns require urgent medical assessment.

  • Sudden, extremely severe headache
  • New headache with weakness or numbness
  • Difficulty speaking or understanding speech
  • Loss of consciousness
  • Seizure
  • Fever with neck stiffness
  • Severe headache after a significant head injury
  • Rapidly worsening or unusual headache
  • New headache with significant visual changes

Headache Treatment

Treatment depends on the type, frequency and severity of headache and the individual's medical history.

Some patients may need treatment for acute attacks, while people with frequent or disabling headaches may require a preventive treatment plan.

Do not repeatedly self-medicate with painkillers without medical advice, especially when headaches are frequent or changing in pattern.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are common migraine symptoms?

Migraine may cause moderate to severe headache, often with nausea or sensitivity to light and sound. Some people may experience visual or other neurological symptoms before or during an attack.

What can trigger migraine?

Triggers vary between individuals and may include lack of sleep, stress, skipped meals, dehydration, hormonal changes and certain foods or environmental factors.

When should a headache be evaluated urgently?

A sudden severe headache, a new headache with weakness or difficulty speaking, loss of consciousness, fever with neck stiffness, head injury or a rapidly worsening headache requires urgent medical evaluation.

Can frequent headaches be treated?

Yes. Recurrent headaches can be evaluated to identify the likely headache type, triggers and contributing factors. Treatment depends on the individual diagnosis and frequency of attacks.
