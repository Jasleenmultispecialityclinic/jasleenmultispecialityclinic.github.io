Frozen shoulder (adhesive capsulitis) causes pain and progressive stiffness of the shoulder joint. Early diagnosis and appropriate treatment help improve shoulder movement and daily function.

Common Symptoms

  • Persistent shoulder pain
  • Progressive shoulder stiffness
  • Difficulty raising the arm
  • Pain while dressing or combing hair
  • Difficulty sleeping on the affected side
  • Reduced range of shoulder movement
  • Pain during daily activities

Risk Factors

  • Diabetes
  • Age above 40 years
  • Previous shoulder injury
  • Long-term shoulder immobilization
  • Thyroid disorders
  • Previous shoulder surgery

Diagnosis

  • Clinical Shoulder Examination
  • X-ray Shoulder
  • Ultrasound (if indicated)
  • MRI Shoulder (selected cases)

Frequently Asked Questions

Can frozen shoulder heal on its own?

Some patients gradually improve over time, but treatment and exercises can reduce pain and help restore movement more quickly.

Is frozen shoulder common in diabetes?

Yes. People with diabetes have a higher risk of developing frozen shoulder.

Will I need surgery?

Most patients improve with medicines, physiotherapy and regular exercises. Surgery is considered only in selected cases when conservative treatment is unsuccessful.
