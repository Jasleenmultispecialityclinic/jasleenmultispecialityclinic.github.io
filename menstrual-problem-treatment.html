What Are Menstrual Problems?

Menstrual problems can include changes in the timing, duration, amount of bleeding or pain associated with periods. Some changes may be temporary, while persistent or significant symptoms may require medical evaluation.

Common Menstrual Problems

  • Irregular periods
  • Heavy menstrual bleeding
  • Painful periods
  • Periods occurring too frequently
  • Periods occurring infrequently
  • Absent periods
  • Bleeding between periods
  • Changes in menstrual pattern

Possible Causes

Menstrual changes can have several causes. These may include hormonal changes, PCOS, thyroid disorders, fibroids, endometriosis, ovarian conditions, pregnancy-related causes, certain medicines and other medical conditions.

Irregular Periods

Irregular periods may occur because of hormonal changes, PCOS, thyroid problems, changes in body weight, stress, excessive exercise or other medical conditions. Persistent irregularity should be evaluated to identify the underlying cause.

Heavy Menstrual Bleeding

Very heavy or prolonged menstrual bleeding can sometimes lead to anemia and weakness. Medical evaluation may be required to determine the cause and appropriate treatment.

Painful Periods

Mild menstrual cramps can be common, but severe or worsening pain, pain that interferes with daily activities, or pain associated with other symptoms may require medical evaluation.

How Are Menstrual Problems Evaluated?

Evaluation depends on age, symptoms, menstrual history, pregnancy possibility, medical history and examination findings. Depending on the situation, a doctor may recommend blood tests, pregnancy testing, hormonal evaluation or pelvic ultrasound.

Menstrual Problem Treatment

Treatment depends on the underlying cause. Depending on the condition, management may include lifestyle measures, medicines, hormonal treatment or treatment of an underlying condition. The appropriate treatment should be selected after medical evaluation.

Menstrual Problems and PCOS

PCOS is a common cause of irregular or infrequent periods. Women with irregular cycles may require evaluation for PCOS and other hormonal or metabolic conditions when clinically appropriate.

Thyroid disorders can sometimes affect menstrual cycles. When symptoms or medical history suggest a thyroid problem, appropriate thyroid testing may be advised.

When Should You Consult a Doctor?

  • Periods remain irregular for several cycles
  • Bleeding is unusually heavy or prolonged
  • Severe menstrual pain affects daily activities
  • Bleeding occurs between periods
  • Periods suddenly stop without an expected reason
  • There is significant weakness, dizziness or symptoms of anemia
  • Menstrual changes are associated with pelvic pain
