An anal fissure is a small tear in the lining of the anal canal. It commonly causes severe pain during bowel movements and may lead to bleeding and muscle spasm. Early treatment helps relieve symptoms and promotes healing.

Common Symptoms

  • Severe pain during bowel movements
  • Bright red bleeding after passing stool
  • Burning sensation after stool
  • Anal itching
  • Visible tear near the anus
  • Constipation due to fear of pain

Common Causes

  • Chronic constipation
  • Passing hard stools
  • Chronic diarrhea
  • Straining during bowel movements
  • Childbirth

Diagnosis

  • Clinical Examination
  • Inspection of the Anal Region
  • Proctoscopy (if required after pain improves)

Frequently Asked Questions

Can an anal fissure heal without surgery?

Yes. Many acute fissures heal with medicines, stool softening, increased fiber intake and adequate hydration. Chronic fissures may require surgical treatment.

Can constipation cause fissures?

Yes. Passing hard stools is one of the most common causes of anal fissures.

How can I prevent fissures?

Drink plenty of water, eat a fiber-rich diet, avoid constipation and do not delay bowel movements.
