Osteoporosis is a condition in which bones become weak and fragile, increasing the risk of fractures. Early diagnosis, lifestyle changes and proper treatment can help maintain bone strength and reduce the risk of broken bones.

Common Symptoms

  • Back pain
  • Loss of height
  • Stooped posture
  • Fragility fractures
  • Bone pain
  • Frequent fractures after minor injuries
  • Reduced mobility

Risk Factors

  • Increasing age
  • Postmenopausal women
  • Low calcium intake
  • Vitamin D deficiency
  • Long-term steroid use
  • Smoking
  • Excess alcohol intake
  • Family history of osteoporosis

Diagnosis

  • Clinical Examination
  • Bone Mineral Density (DEXA Scan)
  • X-ray (if fracture is suspected)
  • Vitamin D Test
  • Serum Calcium Test
  • Blood Investigations (if required)

Frequently Asked Questions

What is osteoporosis?

Osteoporosis is a disease that weakens bones, making them more likely to break.

Who is at higher risk?

Older adults, postmenopausal women, people with vitamin D deficiency and those taking long-term steroids have a higher risk.

Can osteoporosis be prevented?

Healthy nutrition, regular weight-bearing exercise, adequate calcium and vitamin D, and avoiding smoking can help reduce the risk.

When should I get tested?

Your doctor may recommend bone density testing based on your age, medical history and fracture risk.
