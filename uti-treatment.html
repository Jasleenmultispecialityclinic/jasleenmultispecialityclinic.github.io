Urinary Tract Infection (UTI) is one of the most common infections in women. It can affect the bladder, urethra or kidneys. Early diagnosis and proper treatment help prevent complications and recurrent infections.

Symptoms of UTI

  • Burning sensation while urinating
  • Frequent urge to urinate
  • Pain or pressure in the lower abdomen
  • Passing only small amounts of urine
  • Cloudy or foul-smelling urine
  • Blood in urine (sometimes)
  • Fever and chills (in severe infection)

Common Causes

  • Bacterial infection (most commonly E. coli)
  • Poor personal hygiene
  • Holding urine for a long time
  • Pregnancy
  • Diabetes
  • Low immunity
  • Kidney stones
  • Menopause
  • Sexual activity

Diagnosis & Tests

  • Clinical examination
  • Urine Routine Examination
  • Urine Culture & Sensitivity
  • Blood Sugar Test (if required)
  • Ultrasound KUB (if recurrent infection)
  • Complete Blood Count (CBC)

Treatment Options

Treatment depends on the severity of infection and laboratory findings. Early treatment helps prevent kidney infection and recurrent UTI.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can UTI go away without treatment?

Some mild infections may improve, but most UTIs require proper medical treatment to prevent complications and recurrence.

Can men also get UTI?

Yes. Although UTIs are more common in women, men can also develop urinary tract infections.

Can UTI come back again?

Yes. Recurrent UTIs are common in some patients. Identifying the underlying cause and following preventive measures can reduce recurrence.

How can I prevent UTI?

Drink plenty of water, avoid holding urine, maintain good personal hygiene, and seek early treatment if symptoms develop.

When should I see a doctor immediately?

Seek medical attention if you have high fever, severe back pain, vomiting, blood in urine, are pregnant with UTI symptoms, or symptoms persist despite treatment.
