A hydrocele is a collection of fluid around the testicle that causes painless swelling of the scrotum. While many hydroceles are harmless, persistent or large hydroceles may require surgical treatment.

Common Symptoms

  • Scrotal swelling
  • Heaviness in the scrotum
  • Mild discomfort
  • Increase in swelling throughout the day
  • Usually painless swelling

Common Causes

  • Fluid accumulation around the testicle
  • Previous injury
  • Infection
  • Inflammation
  • Congenital hydrocele in children

Diagnosis

  • Clinical Examination
  • Scrotal Ultrasound
  • Transillumination Test
  • Urine Examination (if infection is suspected)

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a hydrocele disappear on its own?

In infants, many hydroceles resolve naturally. In adults, persistent hydroceles usually require medical evaluation and may need surgery.

Is hydrocele surgery safe?

Yes. Hydrocelectomy is a commonly performed procedure with good success rates.

Can hydrocele affect fertility?

Most uncomplicated hydroceles do not affect fertility, but a medical examination is important to rule out associated conditions.
