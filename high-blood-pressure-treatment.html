What Is High Blood Pressure?

High blood pressure, also called hypertension, is a condition in which blood pressure remains elevated over time. Persistent hypertension can increase the risk of heart disease, stroke, kidney disease and other health problems.

Common Risk Factors

  • Family history of hypertension
  • Increasing age
  • Overweight or obesity
  • High-salt diet
  • Physical inactivity
  • Smoking
  • Excessive alcohol intake
  • Long-term stress
  • Diabetes and other medical conditions

Symptoms of High Blood Pressure

High blood pressure often does not cause noticeable symptoms. Some people may experience headache, dizziness or other symptoms, but symptoms alone cannot reliably determine blood pressure.

Regular blood pressure measurement is therefore important, especially for people with risk factors or previously elevated readings.

Blood Pressure Evaluation

Blood pressure should be measured correctly and interpreted in the context of repeated readings and the individual's overall health. Depending on the situation, a doctor may recommend additional blood tests or investigations to assess cardiovascular and metabolic risk.

High Blood Pressure Management

Management depends on the blood pressure level, cardiovascular risk, age, other medical conditions and previous treatment. Depending on the individual situation, management may include lifestyle changes, regular monitoring and prescribed medicines.

Healthy Lifestyle for Blood Pressure

  • Reduce excessive salt intake.
  • Eat a balanced diet rich in vegetables and appropriate whole foods.
  • Maintain a healthy body weight when appropriate.
  • Stay physically active according to your health condition.
  • Avoid smoking.
  • Limit alcohol intake.
  • Maintain adequate sleep and stress management.
  • Take prescribed medicines regularly.

Regular Monitoring

People with hypertension may need regular blood pressure monitoring and medical follow-up. Do not stop or change prescribed blood pressure medicines without discussing it with your doctor.

High Blood Pressure and Diabetes

Hypertension commonly occurs together with diabetes and other metabolic conditions. Managing blood pressure, blood sugar and other cardiovascular risk factors together can be important for long-term health.

When Is High Blood Pressure an Emergency?

Very high blood pressure accompanied by symptoms such as chest pain, severe shortness of breath, severe headache, confusion, weakness, difficulty speaking or sudden vision problems requires urgent medical assessment.
