Common Symptoms of Vaginal Infection

  • Vaginal itching
  • Burning sensation
  • Abnormal vaginal discharge
  • Bad or fishy smell
  • Pain during urination
  • Pain during sexual intercourse
  • Redness and swelling
  • Pelvic discomfort

Common Causes

  • Fungal infection (Candida)
  • Bacterial Vaginosis
  • Poor intimate hygiene
  • Hormonal imbalance
  • Pregnancy
  • Diabetes
  • Use of antibiotics
  • Sexually transmitted infections (selected cases)

Diagnosis & Tests

  • Clinical examination
  • Vaginal discharge examination
  • Urine Routine Examination
  • Urine Culture (if required)
  • Blood Sugar Test
  • Pregnancy Test (if indicated)
  • Pap Smear (selected patients)

Treatment Options

Treatment depends on the underlying cause of infection. Bacterial, fungal and mixed infections require different treatment approaches. Completing the prescribed treatment and maintaining good hygiene helps reduce recurrence.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can vaginal infections go away on their own?

Some mild infections may improve, but persistent symptoms should be evaluated and treated by a doctor to prevent recurrence and complications.

Can diabetes increase the risk of vaginal infection?

Yes. Poorly controlled diabetes increases the risk of recurrent fungal and bacterial vaginal infections.

Can vaginal infections affect pregnancy?

Some infections may affect pregnancy if left untreated. Early diagnosis and treatment are important during pregnancy.

How can I reduce the risk of recurrent vaginal infections?

Maintain good intimate hygiene, avoid unnecessary antibiotic use, control diabetes if present, wear breathable cotton underwear and seek early treatment for symptoms.

When should I consult a doctor immediately?

Consult a doctor if you have severe pelvic pain, fever, foul-smelling discharge, blood-stained discharge, or symptoms that do not improve with treatment.
