What Is PCOS?

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is a common hormonal and metabolic condition that can affect menstrual cycles, ovulation, skin, hair growth and body weight. Symptoms and treatment needs can vary from person to person.

Why Does Lifestyle Matter in PCOS?

Healthy eating habits, regular physical activity, adequate sleep and appropriate weight management can be important parts of PCOS care. Lifestyle recommendations should be individualized according to a person's symptoms, medical history and health goals.

Healthy Eating Approach for PCOS

  • Choose a balanced diet with vegetables, fruits and whole foods.
  • Include adequate protein according to individual nutritional needs.
  • Prefer high-fibre foods such as vegetables, legumes and whole grains.
  • Choose healthy sources of fats in appropriate portions.
  • Limit excessive intake of sugary drinks and highly processed foods.
  • Maintain regular and balanced meals according to your routine.

Physical Activity

Regular physical activity can support overall metabolic health and may be helpful as part of PCOS management. Walking, strength training, cycling, swimming or other suitable activities can be considered according to fitness level and medical advice.

Weight Management

For people with overweight or obesity, even modest weight reduction may improve some metabolic and reproductive features of PCOS. However, PCOS can also occur in people who are not overweight, so treatment should not focus only on weight.

Sleep and Stress Management

Adequate sleep and management of stress are important components of overall health. A consistent sleep schedule, regular physical activity and relaxation techniques may support a healthy lifestyle.

PCOS Treatment Is More Than Diet

Diet and lifestyle are only one part of PCOS management. Depending on symptoms and individual goals, a doctor may recommend evaluation of menstrual irregularities, metabolic health, fertility concerns or other symptoms and may discuss medicines when appropriate.

When Should You Consult a Doctor?

  • Irregular or absent periods
  • Difficulty becoming pregnant
  • Unwanted facial or body hair
  • Persistent acne
  • Unexplained weight changes
  • Symptoms suggestive of hormonal imbalance
