PCOS Diet & Lifestyle Management

Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) is a common hormonal and metabolic condition that can be associated with irregular periods, problems with ovulation, acne, excess hair growth and metabolic risk factors.

Lifestyle management is an important part of PCOS care. A balanced eating pattern, regular physical activity, appropriate weight management and medical follow-up can help address individual symptoms and health risks.

Can Diet Help With PCOS?

There is no single "PCOS diet" that works for every woman. A sustainable, balanced eating pattern is generally more useful than restrictive fad diets or eliminating large groups of foods without a medical reason.

The right approach depends on your symptoms, weight, metabolic health, food preferences, activity level and other medical conditions.

Healthy Eating Pattern for PCOS

A balanced diet can include:

  • Plenty of vegetables
  • Appropriate portions of whole fruits
  • Whole grains and other high-fibre carbohydrate sources
  • Protein-rich foods according to individual dietary preferences
  • Nuts and seeds in appropriate portions
  • Healthy sources of unsaturated fats
  • Minimally processed foods

Foods to Limit

Instead of completely banning specific foods, focus on reducing excessive intake of highly processed foods and added sugars.

  • Sugary drinks
  • Frequent sweets and desserts
  • Highly processed snack foods
  • Excess refined carbohydrates
  • Frequent deep-fried foods
  • Excessively large portions

Individual dietary needs can vary, especially during pregnancy, breastfeeding or when another medical condition is present.

PCOS and Weight Management

For women with overweight or obesity, appropriate weight management may improve some metabolic and reproductive features of PCOS.

Weight goals should be individualized. PCOS care should not be reduced to a number on the weighing scale; menstrual health, metabolic health, nutrition, physical activity and overall wellbeing are also important.

Physical Activity and PCOS

Regular physical activity can support cardiovascular and metabolic health and may be helpful as part of PCOS management.

Choose activities that are realistic and sustainable for you, such as walking, strength exercises, cycling or other suitable forms of exercise. The amount and intensity should be adapted to your fitness and medical condition.

PCOS and Irregular Periods

PCOS can be associated with irregular or infrequent periods because ovulation may not occur regularly. Persistent menstrual irregularity should be medically evaluated rather than treated only with dietary changes.

PCOS and Fertility

PCOS can affect ovulation and may contribute to difficulty conceiving in some women. Appropriate evaluation and treatment can help address fertility concerns.

PCOS and Diabetes Risk

Some women with PCOS have insulin resistance or other metabolic risk factors. Your healthcare professional may recommend assessment of blood glucose or other metabolic markers depending on your individual risk.

PCOS Treatment Is Individualized

Diet and lifestyle are important, but they are not the only components of PCOS care. Depending on symptoms and reproductive goals, medical management may also be considered for menstrual irregularity, acne, excess hair growth, fertility concerns or metabolic risk factors.

Do not start hormonal medicines, supplements or other treatments solely because they are promoted online as a "PCOS cure." Discuss treatment options with a qualified healthcare professional.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can diet help with PCOS?

A balanced eating pattern and healthy lifestyle can support metabolic health and may help manage some PCOS-related concerns. Individual dietary advice should be based on your health and nutritional needs.

What foods are generally suitable for PCOS?

A balanced diet can include vegetables, fruits, whole grains, appropriate protein sources, healthy fats and minimally processed foods. The overall eating pattern is more important than a single food.

Does weight loss help PCOS?

For people with overweight or obesity, appropriate weight management may improve some metabolic and reproductive features of PCOS. Goals should be individualized.

Can PCOS be cured permanently with diet?

There is no single diet that permanently cures PCOS. Lifestyle management, medical treatment when indicated and regular follow-up can help manage symptoms and associated health risks.
