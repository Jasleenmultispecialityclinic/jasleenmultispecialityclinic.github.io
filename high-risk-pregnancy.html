A high-risk pregnancy requires closer monitoring because certain maternal or fetal conditions can increase the risk of complications. Early diagnosis, regular antenatal check-ups and timely treatment help improve outcomes for both mother and baby.

Who Needs High-Risk Pregnancy Care?

  • Pregnancy with high blood pressure (Hypertension)
  • Diabetes during pregnancy (Gestational Diabetes)
  • Thyroid disorders
  • Twin or multiple pregnancy
  • Age below 18 or above 35 years
  • Previous miscarriage or stillbirth
  • Previous Cesarean section
  • Placenta-related problems
  • Preterm labour risk
  • Severe anemia or other medical disorders

Warning Signs During Pregnancy

  • Heavy vaginal bleeding
  • Severe abdominal pain
  • Persistent severe headache
  • Blurred vision
  • Swelling of face or hands
  • High blood pressure
  • Reduced fetal movements
  • Leakage of fluid from the vagina
  • High fever

Investigations & Monitoring

  • Regular Antenatal Check-ups
  • Blood Pressure Monitoring
  • Blood Sugar Testing
  • Thyroid Function Test (TSH)
  • Complete Blood Count (CBC)
  • Urine Routine Examination
  • Ultrasound & Fetal Growth Monitoring
  • Fetal Heart Rate Assessment

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a high-risk pregnancy?

A high-risk pregnancy is one in which the mother or baby has a higher chance of developing complications and requires closer medical monitoring.

Can I have a healthy baby with a high-risk pregnancy?

Yes. With regular antenatal check-ups, proper investigations and timely treatment, many women with high-risk pregnancies deliver healthy babies.

How often should I visit the doctor?

The frequency of visits depends on your condition. Some women may need more frequent check-ups and ultrasound monitoring.

Which medical conditions increase pregnancy risk?

Diabetes, high blood pressure, thyroid disorders, severe anemia, kidney disease, heart disease and multiple pregnancy can increase pregnancy risk.

When should I seek emergency medical care?

Seek immediate care if you have heavy bleeding, severe abdominal pain, leaking of fluid, severe headache, blurred vision, fever or reduced fetal movements.
