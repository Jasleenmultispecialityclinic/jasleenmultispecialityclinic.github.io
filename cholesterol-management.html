What Is Cholesterol?

Cholesterol is a type of lipid that the body needs for normal cell function and hormone production. Different types of cholesterol and other blood lipids have different effects on cardiovascular health.

What Is High Cholesterol?

High cholesterol means that one or more blood lipid levels are above the desirable range. High levels of LDL cholesterol can contribute to the buildup of plaque in blood vessels and increase cardiovascular risk.

Types of Lipids Commonly Measured

  • LDL cholesterol
  • HDL cholesterol
  • Total cholesterol
  • Triglycerides
  • Other calculated lipid measurements when reported

Why Can High Cholesterol Be a Concern?

High cholesterol often causes no symptoms. It may only be detected through a blood test. Over time, unfavorable lipid levels can contribute to cardiovascular disease, especially when combined with other risk factors.

Risk Factors

  • Family history of high cholesterol or cardiovascular disease
  • Diabetes
  • High blood pressure
  • Overweight or obesity
  • Physical inactivity
  • Smoking
  • Unhealthy dietary patterns
  • Some medical conditions and medicines

Lipid Profile Testing

A lipid profile can measure cholesterol and triglyceride levels. The need for testing and the interpretation of results depend on age, medical history and overall cardiovascular risk.

Healthy Lifestyle for Cholesterol Management

  • Choose a balanced diet rich in vegetables, fruits and whole foods.
  • Prefer high-fibre foods and appropriate whole grains.
  • Limit excessive intake of saturated and trans fats.
  • Choose healthier sources of fats when appropriate.
  • Stay physically active according to your health condition.
  • Maintain a healthy body weight when appropriate.
  • Avoid smoking.

Cholesterol and Diabetes

Diabetes can increase cardiovascular risk, so cholesterol management is an important part of overall health care for people with diabetes.

Cholesterol Treatment

Treatment depends on the lipid levels and the person's overall cardiovascular risk. Lifestyle changes may be recommended, and medicines may be considered when clinically appropriate.

Cholesterol-lowering medicines should be taken only as prescribed. Do not start, stop or change a medicine without discussing it with your doctor.

When Should You Consult a Doctor?

  • Abnormal lipid profile results
  • Known high LDL cholesterol
  • High triglycerides
  • Diabetes with cardiovascular risk factors
  • High blood pressure with abnormal cholesterol
  • Family history of premature cardiovascular disease
