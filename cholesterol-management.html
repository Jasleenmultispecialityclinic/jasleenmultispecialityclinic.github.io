Cholesterol Management & Lipid Care

High cholesterol often does not cause noticeable symptoms, but abnormal cholesterol and triglyceride levels can contribute to cardiovascular risk. Proper evaluation includes understanding the lipid profile along with other individual risk factors.

At Jasleen Multi-Speciality Clinic in Behram, patients can discuss cholesterol reports, lifestyle factors, cardiovascular risk and appropriate follow-up with a qualified healthcare professional.

Understanding Your Cholesterol Report

A lipid profile commonly includes several measurements that help assess cardiovascular health:

  • LDL cholesterol: Often referred to as "bad" cholesterol.
  • HDL cholesterol: Often referred to as "good" cholesterol.
  • Triglycerides: A type of fat measured in the blood.
  • Total cholesterol: A combined measure used as part of lipid assessment.
  • Non-HDL cholesterol: Another useful measure in cardiovascular risk assessment.

What Can Increase Cholesterol Risk?

  • Unhealthy dietary patterns
  • Overweight or obesity
  • Physical inactivity
  • Diabetes
  • High blood pressure
  • Smoking or tobacco exposure
  • Family history of cardiovascular disease or inherited lipid disorders
  • Some medical conditions and medicines

Healthy Lifestyle for Cholesterol Management

Lifestyle modification is an important part of cardiovascular risk reduction. A heart-healthy eating pattern generally emphasizes vegetables, fruits, whole grains, healthy protein sources and unsaturated fats while reducing excess saturated fat, added sugars and highly processed foods.

Regular physical activity, maintaining a healthy body weight, avoiding tobacco and getting adequate sleep are also important components of cardiovascular health.

The 2026 American Heart Association dietary guidance continues to emphasize vegetables and fruits, whole grains, healthy protein sources, unsaturated fats and minimally processed foods as part of a heart-healthy dietary pattern. 1

When May Medication Be Needed?

Cholesterol-lowering medication is not decided by a cholesterol number alone. Treatment depends on LDL level, overall cardiovascular risk, diabetes and other medical conditions, previous cardiovascular disease, family history and other factors.

The 2026 ACC/AHA dyslipidemia guideline emphasizes earlier risk assessment, healthy lifestyle measures and individualized lipid-lowering treatment when indicated. Statins remain an important foundation of lipid-lowering therapy, while other medicines may be considered in selected patients. 2

Do not start or stop a cholesterol medicine without discussing it with your healthcare professional.

Cholesterol Testing & Lipid Profile

If your doctor recommends cholesterol testing, a lipid profile can help assess LDL cholesterol, HDL cholesterol, triglycerides and related measurements. Additional testing may be considered depending on individual cardiovascular risk.

If you already have a cholesterol report, bring the report during your consultation so that your results can be interpreted in the context of your overall health.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is high cholesterol?

High cholesterol refers to abnormal levels of cholesterol or other blood lipids/lipoproteins. LDL cholesterol and triglycerides are important factors considered during cardiovascular risk assessment.

Which test checks cholesterol?

A lipid profile or lipid panel is commonly used to assess cholesterol and triglyceride levels. Your doctor may recommend additional testing depending on your health and risk factors.

Can lifestyle changes improve cholesterol?

Healthy eating, regular physical activity, maintaining a healthy weight and avoiding tobacco can support cardiovascular health and may improve lipid levels.

Do all patients with high cholesterol need medicine?

No. Treatment is individualized. Your doctor considers your cholesterol levels, cardiovascular risk, medical history and other risk factors before recommending medication.
