What is PCOS?

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is one of the most common hormonal disorders affecting women of reproductive age. It can cause irregular menstrual cycles, weight gain, acne, excessive facial hair growth, difficulty in conceiving and metabolic problems. Early diagnosis and proper treatment help reduce long-term health risks.

Common Symptoms

  • Irregular or missed periods
  • Difficulty in getting pregnant
  • Weight gain
  • Acne or oily skin
  • Excess facial or body hair
  • Hair fall
  • Dark pigmentation around neck or underarms
  • Mood changes

Causes of PCOS

PCOS is caused by a combination of hormonal imbalance, insulin resistance, genetic factors and lifestyle habits. Early diagnosis and timely treatment can help manage symptoms, improve menstrual cycles and support fertility.

  • Hormonal imbalance
  • Insulin resistance
  • Genetic factors
  • Obesity
  • Sedentary lifestyle
  • Inflammation

Who is at Risk?

  • Family history of PCOS
  • Obesity
  • Diabetes
  • Irregular menstrual cycles
  • Hormonal disorders

Diagnosis & Tests

PCOS is diagnosed based on your medical history, symptoms, physical examination and ultrasound findings. Your doctor may also recommend blood tests to evaluate hormone levels and metabolic health.

  • Pelvic Ultrasound
  • TSH (Thyroid Function)
  • Serum Prolactin
  • LH & FSH
  • Blood Sugar (FBS / HbA1c)
  • Lipid Profile

Treatment Options

Treatment depends on your age, symptoms and future pregnancy plans. Management may include lifestyle modification, weight management, medicines to regulate menstrual cycles, ovulation induction (when pregnancy is desired) and treatment for acne or excessive hair growth. Regular follow-up is important for long-term health.

Diet for PCOS

  • Eat high-fibre foods and fresh vegetables.
  • Choose whole grains instead of refined flour.
  • Include lean protein in every meal.
  • Reduce sugary drinks and sweets.
  • Drink enough water throughout the day.
  • Exercise for at least 30 minutes on most days.

Lifestyle Changes

  • Maintain a healthy body weight.
  • Exercise regularly.
  • Sleep 7–8 hours daily.
  • Reduce stress with yoga or meditation.
  • Avoid smoking and excessive alcohol.

Possible Complications

  • Infertility
  • Type 2 Diabetes
  • High Blood Pressure
  • High Cholesterol
  • Sleep Apnea
  • Endometrial Problems

Can Women with PCOS Become Pregnant?

Yes. Many women with PCOS conceive successfully with appropriate treatment, healthy lifestyle changes and timely medical guidance. Early evaluation can improve the chances of a healthy pregnancy.

When Should You Visit a Doctor?

  • Periods absent for more than 3 months.
  • Difficulty conceiving after trying for several months.
  • Rapid weight gain.
  • Severe acne or excessive facial hair.
  • Heavy or prolonged menstrual bleeding.
  • Persistent pelvic pain.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is PCOS curable?

PCOS can be effectively managed with lifestyle changes, regular follow-up and appropriate treatment.

Can PCOS cause infertility?

Yes. PCOS may affect ovulation, but many women achieve pregnancy with proper treatment.

Can weight loss improve PCOS?

Yes. Even modest weight loss can improve hormone balance and menstrual regularity in many women.

Which diet is best for PCOS?

A balanced diet rich in vegetables, whole grains, lean protein and fibre is generally recommended.

Does every woman with PCOS have ovarian cysts?

No. Diagnosis is based on a combination of symptoms, hormone findings and ultrasound—not ultrasound alone.

Is ultrasound enough to diagnose PCOS?

No. Doctors also consider symptoms, menstrual history and laboratory tests.

Can unmarried women have PCOS?

Yes. PCOS can occur in adolescents and adult women regardless of marital status.

When should I consult a doctor?

If you have irregular periods, infertility, excessive hair growth, acne or unexplained weight gain, seek medical advice.
